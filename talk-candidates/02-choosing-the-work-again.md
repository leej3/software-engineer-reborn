# Candidate B: Choosing the work again

Public talk title: The Software Engineer Reborn: A Vision of Human–AI Collaboration.
Candidate subtitle: Reconsidering a career when the work changes.

Argument: The reasons we chose engineering deserve another look when AI changes its tasks, rewards, and satisfactions.
Sequence: personal values → why the disruption deserves attention → changed work → possible directions → a practical reassessment.
Total: 50 minutes across 13 slides.
First-person language is proposed wording, not a transcript.

## Slide 1

Slide number: 1

Slide title: Why did I choose this work?

Slide content description: Impact, compensation, enjoyment.
Add lifestyle, community, and agency as values John has come to emphasize more.

Slide background/comments: Begin with a recognizable human decision.
Briefly invite people to consider what drew them into engineering, including those now retired.
John should describe his own weighting rather than generalize it to the room.
Source: the latest conversation.
Transition: A good choice under earlier conditions may need reconsideration when those conditions change.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 4 minutes.

## Slide 2

Slide number: 2

Slide title: Why I think this change deserves our attention

Slide content description: State John's belief that AI will transform society.
Show one example of his expanded ability to build complete applications, then identify the broader possibilities for individuals and organizations.

Slide background/comments: Keep the example short; the working method comes later. Ground the belief in the range of useful work he can now attempt. Separate this personal evidence from the larger prediction. Source: [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md).
Transition: Taking the possibilities seriously includes taking costs seriously.

Time: 2 minutes; elapsed: 6 minutes.

## Slide 3

Slide number: 3

Slide title: Optimism includes questions about costs

Slide content description: Show opportunities for useful work alongside resource use, misuse, and the distribution of benefits.
Include a small source citation for the energy example.

Slide background/comments: This is the second and final broad societal context slide.
Use [energy evidence](evidence-notes.md#energy-and-resource-costs); keep water figures and security incidents out until the intended sources are identified.
Mention who pays for infrastructure and who gains as open questions.
Transition: The part of this transformation John can describe most precisely is his own work.

Time: 2 minutes; elapsed: 8 minutes.

## Slide 4

Slide number: 4

Slide title: What engineering used to give me

Slide content description: A difficult problem, sustained effort, a solution understood well enough to explain.
Alongside it: useful work, valued expertise, satisfaction in mastery.

Slide background/comments: Use the source's description of fitting a problem into his head and personally constructing a solution. Keep the career trajectory to one sentence. The aim is to establish why changes in the method can matter emotionally even if the result improves. Source: [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md).
Transition: Describe the gain before naming the loss.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 12 minutes.

## Slide 5

Slide number: 5

Slide title: What I can now attempt

Slide content description: Show a concrete application example from John's source and its path from an idea to a usable artifact.
Annotate the technical boundaries he could cross with assistance.

Slide background/comments: Prefer the car-comparison dashboard because it is accessible and does not require explaining a medical domain. Use an actual artifact if available; do not manufacture a before-and-after benchmark. Explain that new feasible work matters alongside speed on familiar tasks. Source: [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md).
Transition: Show what producing such work requires from him now.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 16 minutes.

## Slide 6

Slide number: 6

Slide title: A short look at my working day

Slide content description: A Codex project, one bounded objective, its generated change, and the evidence John uses to assess it.

Slide background/comments: Proposed seven-minute demo: one minute to orient the room; two to initiate or replay the change; three to inspect output, version history, and a relevant check; one to decide what happens next. Show where context and permission boundaries are established. A prepared completed run is the fallback if generation stalls. Use the demo to let the audience see what John spends attention on; keep detailed model, cloud, cache, and configuration questions for discussion. Source: [development environment](../talk-concepts/06-the-ai-era-development-environment.md).
Transition: Ask what this experience preserves and what it changes about the appeal of the job.

Time: 7 minutes; elapsed: 23 minutes.

## Slide 7

Slide number: 7

Slide title: The work improved; some satisfaction was lost

Slide content description: Expanded capability, less retained understanding, different exhaustion.
Include “Quality ≠ ownership” as a concise statement of the tension.

Slide background/comments: Use John's account of lost mastery and cognitive saturation. Briefly describe the meeting where he could not readily answer questions about a functioning result. Do not assert that everyone feels grief or that a more productive role must feel worse. Make room for relief, enjoyment, indifference, and mixed reactions. Source: [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md).
Transition: Define which part of the earlier arrangement is being reset.

Time: 5 minutes; elapsed: 28 minutes.

## Slide 8

Slide number: 8

Slide title: What needs to start over?

Slide content description: Three reassessments: how I work; what I understand my professional value to be; whether this career still fits my priorities.

Slide background/comments: Distinguish role transformation from an individual's migration into different work.
John's “reset” means revisiting assumptions, with accumulated experience still available.
Someone may retain the same job title through all three changes.
Retired attendees may apply this to projects, mentoring, and communities rather than paid employment.
Source: the latest conversation.
Transition: Career decisions need a sober reading of the external environment as well as introspection.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 32 minutes.

## Slide 9

Slide number: 9

Slide title: What the job evidence can tell us

Slide content description: One dated observation about uneven employment changes; one clearly labeled employment projection; one explicit uncertainty about causation and timing.

Slide background/comments: Use the Stanford and BLS entries in [evidence notes](evidence-notes.md#employment).
Do not compare their numbers as though they measure the same population or period.
Explain why a long-term projection cannot tell an individual whether their present role or entry route remains viable.
John's judgment is to investigate changing expectations early, while avoiding a claim that all layoffs or hiring declines are caused by AI.
Transition: Turn the uncertainty into concrete directions someone could explore.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 36 minutes.

## Slide 10

Slide number: 10

Slide title: Several directions an engineer could explore

Slide content description: Four overlapping directions: broader knowledge work; directing AI-assisted software production; engineering bounded AI systems; conventional engineering where security and direct control matter.
For each, show one typical activity and one question about personal fit.

Slide background/comments: Broader work asks whether one enjoys approaching unfamiliar domains and outcomes. Directing production asks whether repeated specification, review, and experimentation are satisfying. AI systems work asks whether one enjoys evaluation and operational reliability. Conventional engineering asks what depth and control a particular setting requires. These are exploratory directions from John's notes, not job-market rankings or safe occupations. The “nudger” idea can describe the partly empirical feel of agent coordination without claiming that engineering has become unscientific. Sources: the latest conversation and [favorable starting position](../talk-concepts/08-a-favorable-starting-position.md).
Transition: Different directions change how success is judged.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 40 minutes.

## Slide 11

Slide number: 11

Slide title: What would good work mean in those directions?

Slide content description: Compare one artifact with a repeatedly operating system.
Ask whether the evidence supports accepting this result or relying on behavior across many cases.

Slide background/comments: Use an individual comparison report and a repeated report-generation service as illustrative examples. Give the artifact's reviewer sources and checks; give the system's evaluator representative cases and explicit operating limits. This comparison helps someone assess the work they would actually do in a new direction. Compression, assessability, and expert judgment matter in both, but the evidence is organized differently. Source: [assessing quality](../talk-concepts/10-assessing-quality.md).
Transition: A direction should be tested against both its output and its effect on the person doing it.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 44 minutes.

## Slide 12

Slide number: 12

Slide title: Try a direction before committing to it

Slide content description: Choose a small project that matters; define a limit on time and scope; inspect the result with a trusted peer; reflect on enjoyment and agency as well as usefulness.

Slide background/comments: Offer an experiment someone could complete over a week or two if their circumstances allow. Include an experienced developer, a newcomer, and a retired participant as possible collaborators rather than prescribing identical career actions for them. Assess what they learned, what they can explain, what help they needed, and whether they want more of this kind of work. Early action can increase information and options; it does not control employers or the wider economy. Sources: [community](../talk-concepts/05-human-collaboration-and-community.md) and [staying in the loop](../talk-concepts/09-staying-in-the-loop.md).
Transition: Return to the reasons for choosing work on the first slide.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 48 minutes.

## Slide 13

Slide number: 13

Slide title: Reconsider the work with some compassion

Slide content description: Revisit the values from slide 1.
Ask: What do I want to preserve?
What am I willing to learn?
What can I try next?

Slide background/comments: John can express urgency about adapting while acknowledging that enthusiasm, grief, and constraints differ.
Avoid making readiness to adopt AI a test of character.
Close on agency and the ability to revise a choice as evidence changes.

Time: 2 minutes; elapsed: 50 minutes.
