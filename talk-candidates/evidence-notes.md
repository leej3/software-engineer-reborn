## Employment

The August 12, 2026 [Stanford Digital Economy Lab update](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/) reports uneven employment changes by age and AI exposure in ADP payroll data, without evidence of widespread economy-wide displacement associated with AI.
It describes a roughly 19% relative employment shortfall for workers aged 22–25 in highly exposed occupations compared with growth among less-exposed peers, through June 2026.
This is not a statement that AI eliminated 19% of software jobs.
The authors explicitly describe the findings as observational rather than causal and discuss limits to generalization.
If selected, show the date, population, comparison, and limitation together.

The [BLS occupational outlook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) projects 15% growth in combined employment of software developers, quality assurance analysts, and testers from 2024 to 2034.
This is a projection for a grouped occupation over a decade, not a current hiring measure or a guarantee about an individual role.
Do not present it as a direct contradiction of the Stanford result, which concerns a different question and population.

Editorial inference: these sources support a measured discussion of uneven change and uncertainty.
They do not establish which of the four proposed career directions will offer the best compensation or security.
John's recommendation to explore early is a response to uncertainty, not a conclusion mechanically supplied by these statistics.

## Capability and productivity

John's examples are evidence about his experience and the scope of work he can attempt.
Avoid attaching invented multipliers or claiming they establish an economy-wide productivity effect.

The [METR February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) is useful if someone raises the early-2025 finding that experienced open-source developers took 19% longer with AI assistance in that experiment.
The update explains that later measurement was complicated by participation and task-selection effects, and that the resulting data did not reliably estimate current productivity gains.
Use this as a reminder to identify tools, tasks, dates, and measurement limits.
Neither the earlier result nor self-reported gains should be used as a universal account of today's development work.

Keep this methodological detail available for discussion rather than adding another compulsory research slide.

## Energy and resource costs

The [IEA's 2025 Energy and AI executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary) estimates that data centres used about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024, approximately 1.5% of global electricity consumption.
This covers all data centres, not AI alone.
It offers a bounded example of material infrastructure demand without requiring an unsupported per-query estimate.
The report also discusses AI's potential energy-sector benefits; a consumption figure alone does not establish the net climate effect.

For a two-minute context slide, keep the numerical example small and give the rest of the time to John's position on possibilities, risks, and who benefits.
