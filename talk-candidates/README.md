## The intended takeaway

AI has changed what I can do and how I work enough that I think engineers should reconsider their options now.
That reassessment involves both practical choices and the loss of things we valued about the work.
We can explore alternatives while acknowledging uncertainty and treating ourselves and other people with compassion.

This proposed formulation connects John's three aims: appraisal, empathy, and his argument that AI merits serious attention.
It is draft wording for John to assess, not a quotation.
The prediction that AI will transform society remains explicitly his opinion.
Personal examples establish what has changed for him; they do not establish a universal productivity gain or a timetable for job displacement.

## Timing and presentation

Each candidate totals 50 minutes, including its demo, pauses, and transitions.
Aim to finish prepared material at 50 minutes.
Allow approximately 20–30 minutes for discussion, with 90 minutes as the absolute event limit rather than a target.
An interrupted talk should use the candidate's cuts rather than consume the discussion allowance indefinitely.

Slide content descriptions describe what the audience sees.

They are speaker preparation notes, not paragraphs to project or read aloud.

The demo is a proposed run of show, not a claim that a particular artifact or recording has been prepared.

## What has deliberately been deferred

The original risk list could consume much of the talk by itself.
CEO quotations, Bill Gates's statements, the suspected Astra report, an Anthropic security report, the Hugging Face argument, and the Ignacio/Nacho water reference are retained as research leads in [evidence notes](evidence-notes.md).
They have not been guessed into citations or treated as established evidence.
The claims about those sources need exact identification if John chooses to include them.

Employer loyalty cycles, small firms versus established vendors, where profits accumulate, and the opportunity cost of AI investment remain discussion topics.
They can illustrate uncertainty without becoming a forecast that the rest of the talk depends on.
Security-focused conventional engineering is included as a possible direction, without suggesting it is insulated from disruption.
