# Rebuilding Professional Identity — slide draft

Based on the original professional-identity path, with the newer ideas blended in. 27 slides; the four walkthrough slides are prompts for moving through the workspace.
Content is draft slide wording; comments are brief speaker prompts.

## What is actually dying?

### Slide 1: The Software Engineer Reborn

Slide content description:

- A vision of human–AI collaboration.
- What happens to the role—and to the people who chose it?

Slide background/comments: Open with the two meanings of rebirth: a changing profession and a personal transition.

### Slide 2: AI is going to transform society

Slide content description:

- I can take ideas much further than I could before.
- More people can create useful software.
- More kinds of work can become software.

Slide background/comments: The expansion of capability is why I take this seriously.

### Slide 3: Huge possibilities. Consequential choices.

Slide content description:

- What becomes possible?
- Who pays for the infrastructure and resource use?
- Who receives the benefits?
- What risks are we creating?

Slide background/comments: Brief context: optimism, misuse, environmental costs, and the distribution of gains.

### Slide 4: The title can survive while the work changes

Slide content description:

- The role: what we do, what employers need, what earns compensation.
- The person: what we enjoy, what we value, who we understand ourselves to be.

Slide background/comments: A sufficiently changed role can feel like a different profession.

## My trajectory

### Slide 5: How I arrived here

Slide content description:

- Neuroscience → scientific software → infrastructure and backend → AI-assisted full-stack work.

Slide background/comments: Tell the personal trajectory through the changes in what I could do.

### Slide 6: The boundaries of my work expanded

Slide content description:

- Front ends.
  Infrastructure.
  Browser testing.
  Deployment.
- I can carry an idea much further toward a complete product.

Slide background/comments: Use one or two existing artifacts to make that expanded range visible.

### Slide 7: What becomes worth attempting?

Slide content description:

- A useful idea no longer has to justify months of learning a new stack.
- Smaller, more personal problems become worth solving.

Slide background/comments: The car-comparison dashboard is one possible example; choose the artifact I most want to discuss.

## What expansion costs

### Slide 8: Why engineering was satisfying

Slide content description:

- I could immerse myself in a difficult problem.
- The effort produced understanding.
- I could build a solution and explain why it worked.
- That expertise was useful and valued.

Slide background/comments: Establish what the old way of working gave me before describing what changed.

### Slide 9: Expanded capability, lost mastery

Slide content description:

- I can produce work across more domains.
- I retain less of its detail.
- Some of the pleasure of personally solving the problem is gone.

Slide background/comments: Talk about the change in satisfaction and the attempt to develop mastery of this new way of working.

### Slide 10: Quality ≠ ownership

Slide content description:

- The artifact may be better.
- It can still be harder to explain, defend, caveat, or fully stand behind.

Slide background/comments: This is a central tension in the new work.

### Slide 11: The exhaustion is different

Slide content description:

- More possibilities to explore.
- More unfamiliar work to assess.
- More decisions to make.
- A collaborator with no natural stopping point.

Slide background/comments: Connect cognitive saturation to the expansion of work, context, and responsibility.

## Four dispositions

### Slide 12: What does an engineer contribute?

Slide content description:

- Making: How can this be built?
- Directing: What should happen next?
- Deciding: Which outcomes and tradeoffs matter?
- Connecting: What will people understand, trust, and use?

Slide background/comments: These dispositions give us a way to examine the changing balance of our work.

### Slide 13: What we bring with us

Slide content description:

- Decomposing ambiguous problems.
- Working through abstractions.
- Testing assumptions against reality.
- Investigating failures.
- Making systems dependable.

Slide background/comments: Our experience supplies useful starting habits for the transition.

### Slide 14: What we need to develop

Slide content description:

- Understanding the domain and the people affected.
- Choosing worthwhile outcomes.
- Coordinating expertise.
- Communicating intent and uncertainty.

Slide background/comments: AI also lets domain experts approach implementation from the other direction.

## A converging role

### Slide 15: Knowing what matters and making it work

Slide content description:

- Outcome: Does this solve a worthwhile problem?
- System: Can it be operated, evaluated, maintained, and relied upon?

Slide background/comments: Engineers and domain experts increasingly need parts of each other’s expertise.

### Slide 16: Two productive patterns

Slide content description:

- The knowledge worker: use AI to produce a particular artifact.
- The systems engineer: build AI into a capability that performs a class of tasks repeatedly.

Slide background/comments: The distinction helps explain both the opportunities and the work of assessing quality.

### Slide 17: Making an artifact easier to assess

Slide content description:

- Preserve sources, assumptions, calculations, tests, and decisions.
- Make the important questions easy for an expert to investigate.

Slide background/comments: The work includes making the result assessable without independently reconstructing everything.

### Slide 18: Engineering a scalable capability

Slide content description:

- Define a sufficiently bounded task.
- Evaluate behavior across cases.
- Establish operating limits.
- Improve reliability and economics.

Slide background/comments: This is another direction for engineering: turning flexible intelligence into a usable, repeatable service.

## A walkthrough of how I work now

### Slide 19: The code has moved into the background

Slide content description:

- In an IDE, code occupied the center of my attention.
- Now I work through projects, conversations, instructions, artifacts, and decisions.

Slide background/comments: Begin the workspace walkthrough here.
Show the environment as it exists.

### Slide 20: The expanding set of threads I track

Slide content description:

- Several projects.
- Several lines of work within a project.
- Explorations, alternatives, and implementation in parallel.
- Results returning for attention.

Slide background/comments: Show how work branches and how I move between it.

### Slide 21: Compression becomes central

Slide content description:

- What does the agent need to know?
- What do I need to understand?
- What must survive when we return to this work?

Slide background/comments: Show the role of project context, instructions, skills, and short records of decisions.

### Slide 22: How I keep a relationship to the work

Slide content description:

- Artifacts and version history.
- Milestones and review.
- Permissions and execution boundaries.
- Knowing what I am ready to accept.

Slide background/comments: Use the workspace to connect selective ownership with the practical choices I make.
End the walkthrough.

## Deliberate reinvention

### Slide 23: Would I choose this work again?

Slide content description:

- Impact.
  Compensation.
  Enjoyment.
- Lifestyle.
  Community.
  Agency.

Slide background/comments: The disruption prompts a fresh look at the reasons for choosing this career; my priorities have also changed over time.

### Slide 24: Different directions are opening

Slide content description:

- A broader knowledge worker.
- Directing and refining AI-assisted production.
- Engineering bounded, scalable AI systems.
- Conventional development where security and direct control matter.

Slide background/comments: Which activities fit my strengths, interests, and preferred working life?

### Slide 25: We still need other people

Slide content description:

- Expertise beyond our own.
- People who can challenge and assess our work.
- Shared standards.
- Community and belonging.

Slide background/comments: Include the role of experienced and retired engineers in sharing judgment and helping people learn.

### Slide 26: Starting over carries a cost

Slide content description:

- The skills I have accumulated.
- The work I enjoy.
- The identity I have built.
- The future I had expected.

Slide background/comments: Allow room for grief and self-compassion alongside curiosity and excitement.

### Slide 27: Act while there are choices to explore

Slide content description:

- Try the new ways of working.
- Notice what is changing around you.
- Reassess what you want.
- Make room to change direction.

Slide background/comments: Close on agency: bend with the change rather than discovering belatedly that the reasons for choosing the work no longer hold.
