# Candidate A: How my work has changed

Public talk title: The Software Engineer Reborn: A Vision of Human–AI Collaboration.
Candidate subtitle: How my work has changed, and what I am reconsidering.

Argument: My experience of expanded capability and changed responsibility gives me reasons to take AI seriously and reconsider the work I want to do.
Sequence: brief context → personal experience → assessment and demo → work and career choices.
Total: 50 minutes across 13 slides.
All first-person formulations below are proposed wording grounded in John's notes, not verbatim quotations.

## Slide 1

Slide number: 1

Slide title: Why I think AI will transform society

Slide content description: State John's opinion plainly: AI will profoundly change society.
Under it, show three areas of possibility: what individuals can attempt, what organizations can automate, and what becomes affordable to build.
Introduce the talk as a perspective from his own work.

Slide background/comments: Establish the belief without demanding agreement about the speed or destination.
Preview one actual example from the personal source: building a car-comparison dashboard across technical boundaries he previously found costly to cross.
The broader inference belongs to John; the example supplies a concrete reason for it.
Transition: The possibilities are part of the context, alongside the costs.

Time: 2 minutes; elapsed: 2 minutes.

## Slide 2

Slide number: 2

Slide title: Possibilities, risks, and who benefits

Slide content description: Three short prompts: expanded access to useful capabilities; misuse and resource costs; who pays and who receives the gains.
Use one sourced energy example, with the citation visible.

Slide background/comments: Use the IEA item in [evidence notes](evidence-notes.md#energy-and-resource-costs).
Acknowledge security and water concerns as categories without adding unverified incidents or figures.
John can express optimism without claiming that technological progress automatically improves working lives.
Keep organizational forecasts and the longer list of risk references for discussion.
Transition: Narrow from the societal context to something he can describe directly: his working day.

Time: 2 minutes; elapsed: 4 minutes.

## Slide 3

Slide number: 3

Slide title: What I mean by an engineer being reborn

Slide content description: Two distinct questions: What happens to the role?
What happens to the person who chose it?
Below them, distinguish tasks, a paid position, and a professional identity.

Slide background/comments: John sees a sufficiently changed role as potentially unrecognizable, even if the title survives. Make clear that the individual carries experience and values into the transition. The metaphor is a way to ask what must be reconsidered; it is not evidence that all engineering jobs disappear. Source: the latest conversation and [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md).
Transition: Explain the earlier work and its appeal before describing its replacement in his day.

Time: 3 minutes; elapsed: 7 minutes.

## Slide 4

Slide number: 4

Slide title: What changed in the work I could take on

Slide content description: A compact personal trajectory: neuroscience → scientific software → infrastructure and backend → broader application building.
Show a car-comparison dashboard if the original artifact is available.
Describe what John could attempt before and after AI assistance.

Slide background/comments: Use [the abstract](../docs/talk-abstract.md) for the trajectory and the expanded-capability section of [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md) for the example.
Explain one former barrier and how the workflow changed it.
Do not invent development hours, success metrics, or production use.
The point is an expanded feasible range, which is distinct from completing an identical task faster.
Transition: A better result can still leave its producer less prepared to explain it.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 11 minutes.

## Slide 5

Slide number: 5

Slide title: Better work can be harder to stand behind

Slide content description: “Quality ≠ ownership.”
Show three questions: Does it work?
Can I explain its limits?
Am I prepared to stand behind it?

Slide background/comments: Tell the source's meeting example: arriving with a functioning result but needing to walk through the code alongside the person asking basic questions. Do not embellish the meeting, dialogue, or outcome. Distinguish artifact quality from John's understanding, confidence, and willingness to accept responsibility. Sources: [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md) and [assessing quality](../talk-concepts/10-assessing-quality.md).
Transition: This raises two different assessment problems depending on the kind of work being done.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 15 minutes.

## Slide 6

Slide number: 6

Slide title: Two productive patterns, two assessment problems

Slide content description: Compare an expert producing a particular artifact with an engineer building a system that performs a class of tasks repeatedly.
For the artifact: requirements, evidence, and expert review.
For the system: representative cases, operating limits, failure rates, and monitoring.

Slide background/comments: Use the same ordinary domain: evaluating one car-comparison report versus running a service that repeatedly prepares such reports for many people. These illustrative examples are proposed explanations, not claims about John's deployed systems. Both patterns can coexist in a project; AI-assisted construction does not necessarily put AI into the final runtime. The distinction is the object being assessed, not a split between two kinds of people. Source: [assessing quality](../talk-concepts/10-assessing-quality.md).
Transition: Demonstrate the artifact pattern through a bounded development change.

Time: 5 minutes; elapsed: 20 minutes.

## Slide 7

Slide number: 7

Slide title: How I work with an agent now

Slide content description: Live Codex workspace, followed by the resulting artifact and version diff.
Keep a small card visible: objective, constraints, evidence, next decision.

Slide background/comments: Proposed eight-minute demonstration: one minute to show the project and objective; two to initiate or replay a bounded change; three to inspect the preview, diff, and a relevant check; two to explain the acceptance decision and remaining uncertainty. Use a modest change in a prepared project, such as making missing values explicit in a comparison view. Show convenient artifact access and version control as part of the working environment. Inspect configured permission boundaries before running the task. If the agent takes too long, continue with the preserved completed run and identify it as such. Source: [development environment](../talk-concepts/06-the-ai-era-development-environment.md).
Transition: The visible output is only part of the work the human has to manage.

Time: 8 minutes; elapsed: 28 minutes.

## Slide 8

Slide number: 8

Slide title: What takes my attention now

Slide content description: Show one project containing an objective, a short decision record, active work, and an accepted milestone.
Show a second project only to explain the cost of changing context.

Slide background/comments: Explain compression for both participants: the agent needs relevant context; John needs enough understanding to decide what to do next. Project instructions and reusable skills can preserve intent, but summaries can omit a critical constraint. Briefly locate local versus cloud execution and model choice as decisions about where work happens and what tools and context are available. Leave cache mechanics and settings for questions. Sources: [development environment](../talk-concepts/06-the-ai-era-development-environment.md) and [finite human](../talk-concepts/07-the-finite-human-and-the-tireless-producer.md).
Transition: More activity also changes when persistence helps.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 32 minutes.

## Slide 9

Slide number: 9

Slide title: When continuing was the wrong decision

Slide content description: Three stages of the Terraform/OpenTofu example: plausible activity; repeated lack of convergence; discovery of a technological constraint.
End with: stop, investigate, reformulate.

Slide background/comments: The source says John spent days pursuing an outcome that was not achievable with the technology at the time. Keep that historical qualification; do not imply the limitation still exists. Explain how his old instinct to persist needed adjustment. Expertise includes recognizing when to seek another person rather than requesting more generated work. Source: [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md).
Transition: This changes what an engineer contributes, but market consequences require a different kind of evidence.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 36 minutes.

## Slide 10

Slide number: 10

Slide title: What I can infer about jobs—and what I cannot

Slide content description: Three levels: tasks are changing; employers may reorganize work; the eventual number and quality of jobs remain uncertain.
Use one dated labor-market observation with its limitation beside it.

Slide background/comments: Use the Stanford item in [evidence notes](evidence-notes.md#employment).
Keep observations, projections, and John's interpretation distinct.
His practical inference is that people should investigate changing expectations before relying on the old terms of employment.
Discuss broader knowledge work, directing agents, building bounded AI systems, and conventional engineering with strong security requirements as possible directions.
These overlap and carry different demands; none is promised protection.
Transition: A viable direction also has to be a life and working day someone wants.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 40 minutes.

## Slide 11

Slide number: 11

Slide title: Why I chose engineering, and what I value now

Slide content description: Start with impact, compensation, and enjoyment.
Then add John's growing emphasis on lifestyle, community, and agency.
Leave space for attendees to silently identify one thing they value in their own work.

Slide background/comments: Describe satisfaction in solving problems and the value of accumulated mastery, alongside the loss John describes when that mastery feels less scarce. Do not resolve the loss by insisting the new work is equally satisfying. Retired attendees can connect this to earlier transitions, mentoring, or the work they choose outside employment. Sources: the latest conversation and [professional identity](../talk-concepts/03-rebuilding-professional-identity.md).
Transition: Career reassessment needs to include both the opportunities and these costs.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 44 minutes.

## Slide 12

Slide number: 12

Slide title: How I would investigate my next direction

Slide content description: Choose one real task; try an AI-assisted way of doing it; assess the result and the experience; discuss it with someone whose judgment you trust.
Evaluate usefulness, effort, confidence, enjoyment, and control.

Slide background/comments: Present a bounded experiment as a way to learn about fit before making a large career commitment. Reacting early can create options; it is not a guarantee against displacement, nor a reason to blame people with fewer resources to adapt. Community supplies expertise and belonging as well as feedback. If responsibility expands, ask whether the person also has time and authority to inspect, constrain, and stop work. Sources: [community](../talk-concepts/05-human-collaboration-and-community.md) and [staying in the loop](../talk-concepts/09-staying-in-the-loop.md).
Transition: Return to the two meanings of rebirth introduced at the start.

Time: 4 minutes; elapsed: 48 minutes.

## Slide 13

Slide number: 13

Slide title: The role can change; we can reconsider our choices

Slide content description: Three closing statements: take the capability seriously; acknowledge what the transition costs; act while there are choices to explore.
Discussion prompt: Which part of your work would you want to keep?

Slide background/comments: Restate John's position without a prediction of a guaranteed destination.
Experience remains relevant even when an old identity or daily practice needs rebuilding.
Leave the audience with permission to feel differently about the same change and a practical way to investigate it.

Time: 2 minutes; elapsed: 50 minutes.
