# Staying in the Loop

“Human in the loop” often describes something much weaker than meaningful
human control.
An automated system operates at scale while a person is placed downstream to
approve its decisions or absorb responsibility for its failures.
That person may lack the time, knowledge, authority, or practical ability to
intervene.

In that arrangement, the human is not governing the system.
They are functioning as its accountability sink: present enough for an
organization to claim oversight, but not empowered enough to exercise it.

The distribution of power can look like this:

- AI produces the work.
- Management establishes the incentives.
- The organization captures the value.
- The nominal “human in the loop” carries the blame.

This matters to any argument that human value will move toward judgment,
ownership, and responsibility.
Responsibility without corresponding agency is not ownership.
Software engineers should not be encouraged to accept accountability for
systems they cannot understand sufficiently, constrain, interrupt, or refuse.

## Meaningful Participation

A human is meaningfully in the loop only when they can:

- understand the system well enough to judge the consequential parts of its
  behavior;
- observe its effects and receive honest feedback;
- change its objectives or constraints;
- interrupt or refuse its operation;
- exercise those powers with sufficient time and organizational authority;
- decline responsibility when those conditions are absent.

If a person cannot meaningfully say no, they are not in the loop.
They are the liability wrapper around it.

## A Philosophical Meaning

Despite its corporate baggage, *staying in the loop* may also describe a
broader response to uncertainty.
It means refusing both passive displacement and ceremonial supervision:
remaining engaged with change, continuing to learn from reality, and
preserving some causal influence over what happens next.

This sits between two tempting responses:

> **Denial:** This will not fundamentally change me.
>
> **Surrender:** Nothing I choose will matter.
>
> **Agency:** I cannot know the outcome, but I can still participate
> deliberately in determining what happens next.

Feedback loops therefore have both a technical and a philosophical role.
They help us discover when an AI system is wrong, but they also help us orient
ourselves when our models of work, expertise, and professional identity keep
becoming obsolete.

> Feedback loops are not only how we control AI systems. They are how we
> orient ourselves in a world we can no longer predict.

This makes experimentation more than a practical response to uncertain
technology. It becomes part of a broader stance toward change. We do not need
confidence about the destination before we act. We can form a provisional
understanding, make a bounded commitment, observe what actually happens, and
allow the result to change both our next action and our understanding of
ourselves.

Agency without certainty does not eliminate fear, predict which professions
will survive, or guarantee that an experiment will succeed. It rejects the
idea that certainty must precede meaningful action. The alternative to knowing
the future is not surrendering to it; it is maintaining contact with reality
and preserving the capacity to respond.

Staying in the loop does not mean accepting every role assigned to a human by
an automated organization.
It means retaining the knowledge, authority, and practical ability to alter
what happens—and refusing the appearance of control when those conditions do
not exist.

## Possible Role in the Talk

This concept could connect several parts of the talk:

- **Ownership:** Generated work remains governable only when responsibility is
  paired with the power to inspect, constrain, and stop it.
- **Feedback:** Tests, milestones, observation, and review preserve contact
  with reality as production accelerates.
- **Professional identity:** Engineers can participate in change without
  assuming either that their existing role is protected or that their choices
  have become irrelevant.
- **Living with uncertainty:** Agency does not require confidence about the
  destination; it requires the ability to observe, choose, and revise.

The phrase should not be used innocently.
Its compromised corporate meaning is part of the idea and may provide the
reason to introduce it:

> I do not mean “human in the loop” as a person stationed downstream to sign
> their name beneath a machine's decision.
> Staying in the loop means retaining the knowledge, authority, and practical
> ability to change what happens.
> If you cannot say no, you are not in the loop.

The larger question is not merely which tasks humans will continue to perform.
It is whether humans will retain meaningful power within the systems whose
consequences they are asked to own.

## A Recurring Framework, Not Only an Analogy

Milestone-driven development provides a concrete expression of this stance.
A milestone bounds an uncertain effort, creates a moment of observation, and
gives the human an opportunity to recover direction before production
continues. It demonstrates how useful action can proceed without a complete
specification of the destination.

The analogy also has limits. A career, an identity, and a period of social
change cannot be decomposed as cleanly as a software project. The philosophical
idea should therefore remain larger than the development practice. Milestones
are evidence that the stance can work, not a total model for living through
change.

The talk may benefit from expressing the framework clearly and slowly twice:

1. **In the practical middle**, milestone-driven development shows agency
   without certainty at the scale of a project: frame, act, observe, and
   reorient.
2. **Near the conclusion**, the same pattern returns at the scale of a person
   or profession: participate deliberately in change without claiming to know
   where it ends.

The repetition should feel like a widening of meaning rather than a repeated
lesson. The audience first encounters feedback as an engineering mechanism and
later recognizes that it has also been the talk's philosophical framework.

The closing need not promise that everything will be fine. It can make the
more honest claim that uncertainty does not make action meaningless. Our
ability to observe, adapt, judge, and take responsibility matters precisely
because the destination is unknown.
